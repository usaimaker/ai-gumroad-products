# Get Cited by ChatGPT: A 30-Day AI Search Visibility Sprint for Solo Founders

A free, no-budget playbook for making AI assistants name your business when buyers ask them what to use.

## Why this matters in 2026

Buyers no longer scroll a page of blue links. They ask an assistant, read one synthesized answer, and act on it. Google now shows AI generated answers on the majority of searches, ChatGPT is used by hundreds of millions of people as a first stop for recommendations, and a large share of software buyers report using AI search while evaluating vendors.

That creates a brutal new scoreboard. There is no page two in an AI answer. Either the model names you in its shortlist or you are invisible.

The discipline has two halves:

- AEO (Answer Engine Optimization): being surfaced inside Google AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- GEO (Generative Engine Optimization): being cited by chat assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity.

The good news for a one person business: models reward clarity, consistency, and trust far more than budget. A focused operator can own a narrow, specific question that a national competitor can never credibly claim.

## Week 1: Baseline and foundations

You cannot improve a number you have never measured. Spend week one finding out where you actually stand.

- Write down 15 to 20 real buyer prompts. Not keywords, full questions. Examples: best bookkeeper for Etsy sellers, cheapest invoicing tool for freelancers, who should I hire to fix a Shopify checkout.
- Ask every prompt in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode. Record three things per prompt: were you mentioned, were you linked, and which competitors were named instead.
- Log the sources the models cited. A handful of directories, comparison pages, or forum threads usually drive most citations in any category. That list is your target map.
- Claim and fully complete your Google Business Profile if you serve any local or regional market. Every field, correct categories, all services, real hours, fresh photos. For local queries this is still the single highest leverage free asset.
- Fix entity consistency. Your business name, one line description, and service list must be byte for byte identical on your site, social profiles, and every directory. Mismatched details make models hesitant to attribute anything to you.
- Confirm the site is fast and clean on mobile, and that AI crawlers are not blocked in robots.txt. Blocking them is the most common self inflicted wound.

## Week 2: Make your pages quotable

Models lift self contained passages. Marketing fluff is unquotable, so it is unquoted.

- Rewrite your top ten pages so the first paragraph directly answers the question the page exists to answer. Aim for 40 to 60 words, no preamble, no throat clearing.
- Turn headings into the questions buyers actually ask, phrased the way a human would type them into a chat box.
- Build a genuine FAQ page from your inbox. Use the real questions clients send you, and answer each in one tight paragraph before any longer explanation.
- Add structured data: Organization, LocalBusiness or service area business, FAQPage, and Breadcrumb. Only mark up what is visibly on the page.
- Keep facts specific and stable across the site: what you do, for whom, where, and roughly what it costs. Vague ranges get skipped in favor of a competitor who published a number.
- Publish an llms.txt at your site root summarizing who you are, what you offer, and which pages matter most. It costs ten minutes and gives retrieval systems a clean handle on your business.

## Week 3: Earn third party trust

Chat assistants trust what other sites say about you more than what you say about yourself. Mentions come from authority, citations come from extractable pages. You need both.

- Get listed in the specific directories and roundups you saw cited during your week one audit. Go where the models already look rather than where you wish they looked.
- Ask satisfied customers for reviews on a steady cadence rather than in one burst. Velocity reads as a live business, a single spike reads as a campaign.
- Reply to every review, positive or negative. Response behavior is a visible trust signal.
- Pitch one guest article or expert quote per month to a publication your buyers already read. One credible third party mention often outperforms ten self published posts.
- Contribute honest, substantive answers in the communities where your buyers ask questions. Forum threads are cited far more often than most founders expect.

## Week 4: Close gaps and set the loop

- Find your weakest engine. Appearing constantly in Perplexity but never in ChatGPT is a content and authority gap, and it is fixable.
- For every prompt where a competitor was named and you were not, publish one page that answers that exact question better and more specifically than the cited source.
- Re-run the full prompt panel and compare against week one. The absolute score matters little. The direction matters completely.
- Put a recurring 60 minute review on the calendar, monthly. Add new prompts as your offer evolves.

## What to track instead of rankings

- Mention rate: percentage of your prompt panel where a model names you.
- Citation rate: percentage where a model links your site as a source.
- Competitor share: who is named instead of you, and on which prompts.
- Review velocity and branded search volume, both of which tend to move before AI mentions do.

## Realistic expectations

Google surfaces move on normal recrawl timelines, so expect weeks. Assistants that retrieve live from the web can reflect changes quickly. Assistants leaning on training data can lag many months. Optimize for retrieval first, because that is the part you can influence this quarter.

Free tools are enough to establish a baseline, validate that AI search matters in your category, and run spot checks. You have outgrown free only when you need daily tracking across hundreds of prompts and formal attribution reporting. Most solo founders never reach that point.

## How to use

1. Block four 90 minute sessions, one per week, and treat them as client work.
2. Create a simple sheet with one row per buyer prompt and columns for mentioned, cited, competitors named, and sources cited. This sheet is the entire measurement system.
3. Run week one exactly as written before changing a single page. Without a baseline you will not know what worked.
4. Do the work top to bottom. Foundations gate everything else, and GEO tactics on a broken foundation waste effort.
5. If you only have time for five actions, do these: complete the Google Business Profile, add FAQ and Organization schema, collect ten genuine reviews, publish an llms.txt, and test your prompt panel monthly.
6. Re-score quarterly. AI surfaces change fast enough that a passing grade in winter can be a failing one by summer.
