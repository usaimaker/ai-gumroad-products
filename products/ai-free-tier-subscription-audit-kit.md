# The $0 AI Subscription Audit & Downgrade Kit

Find every AI tool you pay for but no longer need, swap it to a free tier in one afternoon, and keep the savings without breaking your workflow. Built for solo founders running on a zero-cost stack.

## What's inside
- A stack inventory sheet
- The keep / swap / cut decision table
- A free-tier swap map for common categories
- 3 copy-paste audit prompts
- A cancellation + data-export script
- A 30-day re-evaluation loop

---

## 1. The problem
Subscriptions accumulate quietly: a trial that converted, a tool you used for one launch, two products with overlapping features. At $12-40 each, a solo stack can quietly cost more than hosting, domains, and ads combined — while 80% of the value comes from three tools.

## 3. Keep / Swap / Cut
| Signal | Decision | Action |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Used in the last 14 days AND no overlap | Keep | Nothing |
| Used in last 14 days but a free tool does the same job | Swap | Export, migrate, cancel before renewal |
| Not used in 30 days | Cut | Export, cancel, remove from bookmarks |
| Only needed for one past project | Cut | Archive output, cancel |
| Two tools overlap | Keep the cheaper one that owns the data | Merge, cut the other |

Rule: if you cannot name the job in one verb, cut it.

---

## 4. Free-Tier Swap Map
| Paid category | Free-first alternative | When the swap is safe |
|---------------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| General AI chat | Free tier of a major assistant | Drafting, outlining, rewriting |
| Automation / integration | Free-tier automation with a task cap | Under the monthly task limit; keep a manual fallback |
| Image / mockup editing | Browser-based free editor | No batch jobs, no brand-critical accuracy |
| Scheduling / social | Native free schedulers | 1-2 channels only |
| Transcription | Free minutes + local fallback | Short files, non-confidential |
| Analytics | Free web analytics | Traffic under the free threshold |

Always check the current free-tier limits before swapping — vendors change caps, and a "free" plan that throttles mid-launch is worse than a $10 bill.

## 5. Three Audit Prompts
**A. Job finder**
> "Here is my tool list with monthly costs. For each tool, infer the single job it performs and flag any two tools that perform the same job. Do not recommend anything yet. List: [PASTE]"

**B. Downgrade planner**
> "For each tool marked SWAP, give a migration plan: what to export, where the data goes, what breaks, and the exact order of steps so I never lose access mid-task. Tools: [PASTE]"

**C. Renewal trap check**
> "Given these renewal dates and notice periods, list the last safe day to cancel each tool and sort by urgency. Dates: [PASTE]"

---

## 6. Cancellation Script (send before you cancel)
1. Export everything: settings, prompts, outputs, and client files.
2. Save exports to a local folder named `tool-name-YYYY-MM-DD`.
3. Confirm the export opens outside the tool.
4. Change any automation/webhook pointing at the tool to the replacement.
5. Cancel with this line: "Please cancel my subscription at the end of the current billing period and confirm in writing."
6. Remove the saved card only after written confirmation.
7. Log the annualised saving in your inventory sheet.

---

## 7. The 30-Day Loop
| When | Action |
|------|--------|
| Day 0 | Run the audit, cut and swap |
| Day 7 | Check every swapped workflow still runs |
| Day 14 | Note any friction; revert one swap if needed |
| Day 30 | Re-run the inventory; cancel anything re-added by habit |

Repeat quarterly. A zero-cost stack is a maintenance habit, not a one-time cleanup.
