# The Free Meeting Memory Engine: A Solo Founder's Zero-Dollar Note Stack for 2026

Turn every call into tracked action items without paying for another SaaS seat. This guide maps the real 2026 free tiers of AI meeting notetakers and shows how to chain them into your CRM, tasks, and follow-ups for $0.

## Why meetings leak value

Most solo founders sit in 5 to 15 calls a week: sales demos, client check-ins, supplier negotiations, investor updates. The cost is not the call itself. It is the 20 minutes after, when the action items, decisions, and promises evaporate into a chat thread nobody reopens. Paid notetaker suites fix this but add a recurring bill you do not need yet. The free tiers in 2026 are strong enough to build a memory engine yourself if you pick the right tool and add a small habit.

## The 2026 free-tier reality (checked August 2026)

Free plans split into two groups: genuinely usable, and a trial wearing a free badge. Limits change often, so confirm the live pricing page before you commit, but here is the current picture from multiple reviews:

- Fathom: unlimited recordings, transcription, and storage on free. Advanced AI summaries (action items, follow-up email drafts, Ask Fathom) cap at about 5 calls per month. Deepest on Zoom; Meet and Teams work but some advanced features sit behind paid plans. No bot joins your call on the bot-free beta mode.
- Fireflies: unlimited transcription, 800 minutes of stored audio per seat, and about 20 AI credits per month (each summary or action-item extraction draws from that pool). Strong free integrations: Salesforce, HubSpot, Notion, Slack, Zapier.
- tl;dv: unlimited recordings in 30 plus languages, but auto-deletes after about 3 months and caps AI notes at roughly 10 meetings per month. Good clipping and sharing.
- Otter: 300 minutes per month total, with 3 file imports. Best English transcription accuracy and speaker identification in the free tier, but the minute cap is tight for daily users.
- Granola: about 25 meetings on free, then paid. No bot, captures your computer audio locally, and expands your rough notes into a full summary. Mac-first; Windows support limited in 2026.
- Krisp: 60 minutes of AI transcription per day, resetting every 24 hours, plus the best noise cancellation in the category. Good if you run one or two calls a day from a noisy space.

The pattern: none of these alone is a memory system. They capture. You still have to route the output into action.

## Pick your notetaker in 60 seconds

Answer three questions, then choose:

1. Which platform do you live on? Zoom-heavy and want unlimited free — pick Fathom. Mixed Zoom and Meet with a need to clip and share — tl;dv. Google Meet or Teams as your main room — verify Fathom's current feature matrix, or use Granola for a bot-free local capture.
2. Do you need CRM sync on day one? Fireflies free connects to Salesforce, HubSpot, and Notion without payment, which matters for sales founders.
3. Is transcription accuracy the priority? Otter wins for English, but watch the 300-minute ceiling.

If you are a Mac user who dislikes bots in the room, Granola's local capture is the quietest option. If you run back-to-back calls daily, Otter and Granola will hit their caps fast — Fathom or tl;dv fit better.

## Chain notes into action for $0

The engine is the habit, not the app. After each key meeting:

- Open the AI summary and confirm the action items are correct. Edit any the model misheard.
- Push each action item into a free task tool (Trello free, Notion free, or a Google Sheet). One row per item, with owner and due date.
- Paste the key decision into your CRM note or a simple deals sheet so the context survives.
- Use the notetaker's follow-up email draft (Fathom, Fireflies) as your first reply, then personalize in 30 seconds.

For a little automation without a bill, the free Zapier plan (about 100 tasks per month, two-step Zaps) can append new Fireflies or Fathom summaries to a Google Sheet or Notion database automatically. That is enough to keep a rolling meeting log you review each Friday. Stay on the two-step limit: trigger plus one action. Multi-step logic is where paid tiers begin.

## The Google Meet bot roadblock

Since March 2026, Google has placed third-party meeting bots in a risk queue that requires the host to manually approve them before they join. This broke the old set-and-forget workflow for Meet users. Two free routes around it:

- Use a notetaker with bot-free capture. Fathom's bot-free beta records from your device, and Granola captures local audio, so no approval prompt appears.
- If you must use a bot-based tool on Meet, pre-approve it as the host or join the call yourself and let the bot in. For client calls where you are a guest, prefer the local-capture tools.

## Your 15-minute Monday setup

- Install one notetaker matched to your main platform. Enable AI summaries.
- Connect it to a free task tool or sheet using the native integration or a two-step Zapier Zap.
- Write a one-line post-meeting rule somewhere visible: summarize, then push action items within 10 minutes.
- Set a 15-minute Friday block to search across all meetings for open threads and clear them.

## Zero-pay guardrails

- Stay on free tiers by matching the tool to your real platform instead of forcing one app everywhere.
- Use the free Zapier only for simple two-step flows; do not build multi-step logic you cannot sustain.
- Watch the caps: Otter 300 minutes, Granola 25-meeting lifetime, Fireflies 20 AI credits. When you blow past these every week, that is your signal to pay — not before.
- Pay only when AI summaries become a daily need, multi-platform CRM sync is required, or meeting volume routinely exceeds what the free tier allows.

## Quick-start checklist

- Choose notetaker by main platform (Fathom for Zoom, Fireflies for CRM, Granola for bot-free, Otter for accuracy).
- Turn on AI summaries and action-item extraction.
- Connect to a free task tool or sheet.
- After each key call: confirm items, push to tasks, log the decision, draft the follow-up.
- Friday: sweep all meetings for open threads and close them.

Build the habit before the bill. A free notetaker plus a 10-minute routing routine beats an expensive suite you never fully adopt.
